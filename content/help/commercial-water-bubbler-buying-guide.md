Choosing a **commercial water bubbler** is mostly a site-design decision. The best unit is not simply the one with the highest specification or the largest cabinet. It is the one that matches **where it will be installed, how people will use it and when demand peaks**.

For most Australian schools, offices, factories, warehouses, gyms and public facilities, work through these decisions in order:

1. Is the location indoor, sheltered or fully exposed outdoors?
2. Do users need direct drinking only, or bottle filling as well?
3. What does peak demand look like at the busiest time of day?
4. Do you need chilled water, filtration, or both?
5. Is the product appropriately certified for the intended plumbing application?
6. Is there a practical water connection, drainage route and power supply?
7. Can filters and internal components be accessed easily for future servicing?
8. Would one station be enough, or would multiple stations better serve the site?

If you already know those answers, compare the three current models on the [commercial water bubbler hub](/commercial-water-bubblers).

## 1. Start with the installation environment

The first question is not capacity. It is **where the unit will physically live**.

### Indoor or sheltered commercial locations

For offices, foyers, school corridors, staff areas, sheltered factory floors and similar locations, a stainless-steel cabinet is a strong starting point.

The Enviro Aqua round and square commercial models use **SUS304 stainless-steel cabinets** and are intended for indoor or sheltered positions.

Choose between them based mainly on dispensing format:

- the **round model** is the simpler bubbler-only option;
- the **square model** adds a separate side tap for bottles, glasses or jugs.

### Exposed outdoor locations

For schoolyards, external factory areas, sporting facilities and other locations exposed to weather, choose a cabinet specifically suited to that environment rather than assuming an indoor cabinet can simply be moved outside.

The Enviro Aqua outdoor model uses a **rust-free, UV-stable HDPE cabinet**. That makes it the relevant starting point where weather exposure is a primary design consideration.

Outdoor planning should also consider:

- where water can drain safely;
- protection and suitability of the electrical supply;
- sun and weather exposure;
- cleaning conditions;
- access for filter changes and servicing;
- how plumbing reaches the unit.

## 2. Decide whether bottle filling matters

A **water bubbler** lets someone drink directly from an upward-flowing spout. A **bottle-filling outlet** gives users a separate way to fill reusable bottles, cups or jugs.

The right choice depends on behaviour at the site.

Bottle filling is particularly useful where users commonly arrive with their own drink bottle, including:

- schools and education facilities;
- gyms and sporting facilities;
- factories and warehouses;
- offices and staff workplaces;
- community and public facilities.

If most people only need a quick drink, a bubbler-only station can be simpler. If reusable bottles are common, a combination unit usually gives the station more flexibility.

In the Enviro Aqua range, the **square stainless** and **outdoor HDPE** models include a separate side tap. The **round stainless** model is the simpler direct-drinking option.

## 3. Size for peak demand, not just headcount

Commercial bubbler capacity is commonly expressed in **litres per hour of chilled output**. That number is useful, but it should not be converted into a simplistic rule such as “one bubbler per X people”.

Two sites with the same number of users can have very different demand patterns.

A 100-person office may spread water use across the entire day. A school may have a large proportion of students drinking during a short recess. A factory may concentrate demand around a shift change. A gym may see clusters around classes or evening training.

That means the better questions are:

- How many people may want water at the same time?
- Are people taking quick drinks or filling large bottles?
- Is there one central station or several convenient stations?
- How long does the unit have to recover between peaks?
- Would queues be a problem if everyone uses one location?

All three Enviro Aqua commercial bubblers are rated at **20 L/hr chilled output**. If a site has unusually high simultaneous demand, multiple stations can be a better design response than relying on one central unit.

## 4. Choose chilled, filtered or both

A commercial bubbler can perform two separate functions:

**Chilling** reduces the temperature of incoming water before dispensing.

**Filtration** treats the incoming water before it reaches the drinking outlet.

The current Enviro Aqua commercial range combines both. Each model provides approximately **8–12 °C chilled water** and uses **sediment plus carbon filtration**.

The sediment stage helps capture particulate matter before it reaches the carbon stage. The carbon stage is primarily used to improve drinking-water taste and odour.

The range uses **standard 10-inch filter housings**, which makes future cartridge sourcing and servicing simpler than a proprietary sealed cartridge format.

Filter life should be based on the cartridge specification, incoming water quality and actual usage rather than an arbitrary calendar interval.

## 5. Check WaterMark and product certification

For an Australian commercial plumbing project, certification should be checked against the **exact product being specified**, not assumed from the product category or supplier name.

The WaterMark Schedule of Products includes **drinking fountains and bottle fillers** as cold or chilled water dispensing apparatus. Current requirements should be checked when specifying or installing a product, because certification schedules and referenced specifications can be updated.

The three current Enviro Aqua commercial bubbler models are listed on their product pages as **WaterMark certified under licence 23484**.

For more detail on the scheme, see [WaterMark certification explained](/help/watermark-certification-explained/).

## 6. Check the site before buying

A commercial bubbler is not just a freestanding appliance that can be placed anywhere. Before ordering, confirm how the unit will connect to the building.

A site assessment should consider:

- **Mains water:** where the cold-water supply will come from.
- **Drainage:** how waste water from the drinking bowl will discharge.
- **Power:** whether a suitable supply is available for the chiller.
- **Floor and wall position:** whether the cabinet can be positioned securely and serviced later.
- **Access:** whether filters and internal components remain reachable.
- **Outdoor exposure:** whether plumbing and electrical work are appropriate for the environment.

Use an appropriately licensed plumbing practitioner for the relevant mains-water and drainage work, and have the installer confirm local requirements for the project.

## 7. Think about maintenance before installation

A station that looks convenient on a plan can become difficult to maintain if the filter housing or internal components cannot be reached after installation.

Before finalising the location, check:

- how the cabinet opens;
- where the filter housing is located;
- whether a technician can isolate the water supply;
- whether cartridges can be removed without moving the entire unit;
- whether the drain can be inspected and cleaned;
- whether surrounding surfaces can be cleaned easily.

For high-use sites, maintenance access is part of the buying decision, not an afterthought.

## 8. One station or multiple stations?

One higher-traffic station is not always better than two conveniently located stations.

Multiple stations may make sense where:

- different buildings or work zones are separated;
- a school has several high-traffic areas;
- a warehouse has distant work zones;
- a gym has distinct training areas;
- users would otherwise queue during short peak periods;
- one station would be inconvenient for part of the site.

This is why peak usage and physical layout should be considered together.

### Schools

Prioritise durability, placement, bottle-filling behaviour, outdoor exposure and peak demand during breaks. Stainless models suit sheltered areas; the HDPE model is the starting point for exposed outdoor positions.

## Commercial water bubbler comparison checklist

Before choosing a unit, confirm each of these points:

- installation location: indoor, sheltered or exposed outdoor;
- cabinet material;
- direct-drinking spout;
- bottle-filling outlet if required;
- chilled-water output in litres per hour;
- expected peak demand;
- filtration stages;
- replacement-filter format;
- WaterMark details for the exact model;
- mains-water connection;
- drainage route;
- electrical supply;
- service and filter access;
- whether one or multiple stations are required.
