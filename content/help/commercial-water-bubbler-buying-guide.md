Choosing a **commercial water bubbler** is mostly a site-design decision. The best unit is the one that matches **where it will be installed, how people will use it and when demand peaks**.

For most Australian schools, offices, factories, warehouses, gyms and public facilities, work through these decisions in order:

1. Is the location indoor, sheltered or fully exposed outdoors?
2. Do users need direct drinking only, or bottle filling as well?
3. What does peak demand look like at the busiest time of day?
4. Do you need chilled water, filtration, or both?
5. Is the product appropriately certified for the intended plumbing application?
6. Is there a practical water connection, drainage route and power supply?
7. Can filters and internal components be accessed easily for future servicing?
8. Would one station be enough, or would multiple stations better serve the site?

If you already know those answers, compare the three current models on the [commercial water bubbler hub](/commercial-water-bubblers).

## 1. Start with the installation environment

For offices, foyers, school corridors, staff areas, sheltered factory floors and similar locations, a stainless-steel cabinet is a strong starting point. The round and square commercial models are intended for indoor or sheltered positions.

For schoolyards, external factory areas, sporting facilities and other locations exposed to weather, choose a cabinet specifically suited to that environment. The **YL-600P Water Bubbler Grey** uses a rust-free, UV-stable HDPE cabinet and is the outdoor-focused model in the range.

## 2. Decide whether bottle filling matters

A **water bubbler** lets someone drink directly from an upward-flowing spout. A **bottle-filling outlet** gives users a separate way to fill reusable bottles, cups or jugs.

The **YL-600C Water Bubbler Square** and **YL-600P Water Bubbler Grey** include a separate side tap. The **YL-600R Water Bubbler Round** is the simpler direct-drinking option.

For a deeper comparison, see [water bubbler vs water cooler](/help/bubblers-vs-coolers/).

## 3. Size for peak demand, not just headcount

Commercial bubbler capacity is commonly expressed in **litres per hour of chilled output**. Do not convert that into a simplistic rule such as “one bubbler per X people”. Two sites with the same number of users can have very different demand patterns.

All three Enviro Aqua commercial bubblers are rated at **20 L/hr chilled output at approximately 8–12 °C**. If a site has unusually high simultaneous demand, multiple stations can be a better design response than relying on one central unit.

## 4. Choose chilled, filtered or both

**Chilling** reduces the temperature of incoming water before dispensing. **Filtration** treats the incoming water before it reaches the drinking outlet.

All three Enviro Aqua models provide chilled, filtered drinking water. The **YL-600C square model** uses sediment plus activated carbon filtration, the **YL-600P HDPE model** uses PP sediment plus activated carbon filtration, and the **YL-600R round model** uses an integrated drinking-water cartridge.

Filter life should be based on the cartridge specification, incoming water quality and actual usage rather than an arbitrary calendar interval.

## 5. Check WaterMark and product certification

For an Australian commercial plumbing project, certification should be checked against the **exact product being specified**, not assumed from the product category or supplier name.

## 6. Check the site before buying

Before ordering, confirm the mains-water supply, drainage route, power supply, cabinet position, service access and whether the installation is indoor, sheltered or exposed outdoor. Use an appropriately licensed plumbing practitioner for the relevant mains-water and drainage work.

## 7. Think about maintenance before installation

Before finalising the location, check how the cabinet opens, where the filter is located, whether the water can be isolated, whether cartridges can be changed without moving the entire unit and whether the drain can be inspected and cleaned.

## 8. One station or multiple stations?

Multiple stations may make sense where buildings or work zones are separated, a school has several high-traffic areas, a warehouse has distant work zones, a gym has distinct training areas or users would otherwise queue during short peak periods.
