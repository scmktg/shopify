## Water on the coastal strip

Mains water is supplied by Central Coast Council. The source water comes from Mangrove Creek Dam in the hinterland, treated and chlorinated to meet the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines, and delivered at standard mains pressure. Chloramine is not used on the Central Coast.

The water arriving at a Terrigal or Kincumber tap is the same supply that reaches every other Central Coast suburb. The only difference is the run of pipework. The most common complaint is the chlorine taste — and a basic carbon under-sink filter or whole-house system removes it.

## Beachside considerations

The water in your pipes is not saltier because you live near the beach. Brisbane Water is estuarine, the Pacific is ocean, and neither feeds the mains supply. What the coast does affect is the hardware outside your house:

- **Outdoor taps and hose connections** corrode faster on beachside blocks. Specify marine-grade brass or 316 stainless steel.
- **Outdoor shower heads** are worth upgrading to 316 stainless — chrome plates pit and flake within a couple of summers.
- **Pool top-up and irrigation lines** that run through filter housings should use UV-stable PEX or copper, not standard polyethylene.

The internal filter housings themselves sit inside the house and are not affected by salt aerosol.

## Recommended setups

- **Standard family home, mains water:** two-stage sediment + carbon under-sink for the kitchen drinking tap. About $200 for the system, six to twelve months between cartridge changes.
- **Beachside premium home:** three-stage 20" Big Blue whole-house system. Filters every outlet, including the shower — useful after a beach session when you want chlorine off the skin in addition to the salt.
- **Holiday rental:** keep it simple. Single-stage carbon under-sink. Cleaners can change cartridges between bookings in five minutes.
