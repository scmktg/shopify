## Water on the Peninsula

Mains water is supplied by Central Coast Council. The source is Mangrove Creek Dam, treated and chlorinated to meet the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines, with the same chemistry that reaches the rest of the Coast. Chloramine is not used.

The water arriving at the meter is clean. What changes the taste and appearance at the kitchen tap, especially in the older suburbs, is the pipework between the meter and the sink — older mains in the street, and older internal house plumbing.

## Older homes and discoloured water

Woy Woy, Ettalong, Booker Bay, and parts of Umina have a lot of pre-1980 fibro and weatherboard housing stock. Many still have original galvanised internal plumbing. The mains arriving at the meter is fine, but galvanised pipework slowly sheds iron oxide and sediment, especially after upstream maintenance or a water-main flush.

The standard signs are occasional brown water at the cold tap, sediment in the kettle, or a metallic taste. The fix is a sediment pre-filter sized to the build-up:

- **Single under-sink tap:** a 10" 5 micron sediment cartridge ahead of a carbon block, in a twin housing. About $200 fitted by a plumber.
- **Whole house:** a 20" Big Blue sediment cartridge as the first stage, with carbon downstream. Sized correctly it handles the whole property and keeps sediment out of the hot-water system as well.

If the discolouration only shows up after Council does work on the street main, it usually clears in a day or two — but a sediment filter stops it reaching your taps in the meantime.

## Beachside fittings

Umina, Ocean Beach, Ettalong, and Pearl Beach get serious salt aerosol. The water itself is not affected, but outdoor taps, hose bibs, and outdoor shower hardware corrode quickly. Specify 316 stainless steel or marine-grade brass on anything exposed.
