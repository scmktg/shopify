  body: |
    An under-sink filter is the standard upgrade for Australian households that want better-tasting drinking water without re-plumbing the whole home. The system mounts inside the kitchen cabinet, taps off the cold-water line, and delivers filtered water through either the existing kitchen mixer or a dedicated filtered-water tap. Choosing the right system comes down to four questions: what you want removed, how much flow you need, whether you want a dedicated tap, and what cartridge lifecycle suits your household.

    ### How under-sink filters work

    Cold mains water enters the filter housing through a 1/4" or 3/8" line tapped off the cold supply with a self-piercing or compression tee. Inside the housing, water passes through one or more cartridges — sediment first, then carbon, then specialty media in multi-stage systems — and exits to either the kitchen mixer or a separate filtered-water tap on the bench. Most systems run at full mains pressure, which means flow at the outlet matches the rest of the kitchen. Reverse-osmosis systems are the exception — they push water through a semipermeable membrane at a slower rate, which is why RO setups include a small holding tank to even out demand.

    ### Single-stage vs multi-stage

    A single-stage carbon filter is the simplest option. It removes chlorine, the chlorine taste, sediment from old pipes, and most organic contaminants. It is enough for the majority of homes on town water where the only complaint is taste. Multi-stage systems add a dedicated sediment pre-filter (catches fine grit before it loads up the carbon), additional carbon stages with finer micron ratings, or specialty media like alkaline, fluoride-removal, or ultrafiltration. If you are on town water and the goal is "make it taste better," one or two stages is enough. If you are on tank water, bore water, or you specifically want to reduce fluoride or heavy metals, you want three or more stages — and for the strictest contaminant reduction you want reverse osmosis.

    ### Choosing the right cartridge

    Cartridges are sized in two dimensions: length (10" or 20") and diameter (2.5" or 4.5" — the 4.5" big-blue housings handle more flow and last longer between changes). The micron rating tells you the smallest particle the cartridge captures — a 5-micron sediment filter catches anything down to 5 microns, a 1-micron is finer, a 0.5-micron finer again. Carbon cartridges are rated by media type (granular activated carbon, or carbon block) and capacity. For a standard 10" x 2.5" under-sink setup, expect a sediment + carbon pair to last 6–12 months for a family of four.

    ### Installation: DIY or plumber

    Under-sink filters with self-piercing or push-fit fittings are well within reach of a confident DIYer. The job takes 20–40 minutes and needs nothing more than a shifter, the included drill bit, and the existing isolation valve under the sink. If you are installing a dedicated filtered tap, you need a 12mm hole in the benchtop — stainless steel is easy, stone is a job for a plumber with a diamond hole saw. WaterMark certified housings can be installed by a licensed plumber if you want the install signed off for council compliance; for an internal under-sink filter on a cold line, most households self-install.

    ### Maintenance and replacement intervals

    Cartridges are consumables, not lifetime parts. Sediment filters typically run six months on town water and three months on tank water; carbon filters run twelve months on light use and six months on heavy use; reverse-osmosis membranes last 2–3 years. The housing itself lasts indefinitely. Mark the install date inside the cabinet door with a sticker, or set a calendar reminder — a clogged sediment filter shows up as reduced flow, but a spent carbon filter does not give a visible signal and just stops working. Replacement cartridges are kept in stock for every system we sell.

Drinking water straight from the kitchen tap, with the chlorine, sediment, and odd taste filtered out before it reaches the glass. That is what an under-sink filter does: a small, plumbed-in system that lives inside the kitchen cabinet and runs the cold-water supply through one or more replaceable cartridges. You do not see it, you do not hear it, and it does not change the rest of the kitchen tap's flow.

Under-sink is the right choice when you want filtered drinking water specifically — not whole-of-house filtration, which is a different problem. The whole-house systems we stock under [whole-house filters](/water-filters/whole-house/) sit on the mains supply at the meter and filter every tap and shower. An under-sink system is targeted at the kitchen sink, so the cost of the cartridges is lower and the install is straightforward. For most Australian households on town water, an under-sink filter is the upgrade that makes the biggest difference for the least money.

What you choose between depends on what is in the water and what you want to remove. A single-stage carbon filter handles chlorine, chlorine by-products, and the taste that comes with municipal supply — a one-cartridge system that takes care of the most common complaint. A two- or three-stage system adds a sediment pre-filter (catches grit before it loads up the carbon) and, optionally, a specialty stage: alkaline for pH balancing, fluoride-removal media, or ultrafiltration for cyst and bacteria reduction. At the top of the range, reverse osmosis pushes water through a semipermeable membrane that removes the broadest range of contaminants — including dissolved solids that carbon cannot touch — and stores the filtered output in a small holding tank under the bench.

Every WaterMark certified system on this page is labelled with its licence number under the product specifications. WaterMark applies to anything plumbed into mains-pressure supply, so the housings and fittings need it; cartridges themselves are consumables and are not separately certified. If you are installing on a non-mains source (tank, bore, caravan), non-certified housings are perfectly legal and usually cheaper — those are also clearly labelled.

Replacement cartridges are stocked for every system we sell. We do not discontinue a housing without keeping at least three years of cartridge stock available afterwards. Choose your system on the basis of what it filters and how often you want to swap cartridges — not on whether the cartridges will be available next year.
